1 - Automation and AI-Driven Morphological Profiling of Organoids for Drug Discovery
Presenter's name
Tam Nguyen
Company/Institution
AstraZeneca
Category
Advancements in Image-Based AI & Foundation Models for Drug Discovery
Three-dimensional organoid models of breast and lung cancer now enable more physiologically relevant drug assessment, particularly for agents targeting DNA-damaging and apoptotic mechanisms. However, robust phenotypic profiling of these organoids requires methods that are quantitative, highly reproducible, and scalable. Leveraging the label-free, non-invasive capabilities of brightfield imaging, we established an automated, high-throughput workflow utilizing the Operetta CLS high-content imaging system and Arivis AI- driven analysis software. High-content imaging provides rapid, standardized acquisition of high-resolution brightfield datasets across extensive organoid cohorts, eliminating staining artifacts and batch variability. Automated image processing with Arivis employs deep neural networks for precise organoid segmentation, detailed morphometric feature extraction, and identification of drug-induced morphological changes —including nuclear fragmentation, condensation, and growth arrest —across a wide range of experimental conditions. This integrated system supports label-free, multiparametric phenotyping at scale, delivering objective and consistent quantification essential for differentiating responses for DNA-damaging and apoptotic drugs of interests. By enabling high-throughput image capture and unbiased AI- powered analysis, this platform markedly accelerates drug screening while minimizing subjective operator influence and manual workload. Our automated workflow facilitates discovery and prioritization of candidate therapeutics for breast and lung cancers, and is readily adaptable to diverse organoid models and drug mechanisms. Continued development will expand its application to additional imaging modalities and disease contexts, supporting the next generation of preclinical phenotypic drug discovery.

Category

Designing image analysis pipelines in CellProfiler typically demands expert knowledge and extensive manual tuning, creating barriers to scalability and accessibility in high-throughput biological research. In this proof-of-concept study, we introduce an agentic workflow that harnesses in-context learning and multimodal AI models to automatically generate CellProfiler pipelines from minimal user input. 4 - Enabling Ultra-High Throughput Cell Painting for Drug Discovery
Presenter's name
Victoria Arinella
Company/Institution
UF Scripps
Category
Advancements in Image-Based AI & Foundation Models for Drug Discovery
Morphological screening is a powerful tool with the potential to narrow down mechanisms of actions in an unbiased manner, predict the toxicity of compounds, and contribute to the development of more first-in-class medicines, thereby offering potential for an improved understanding of diseases. The Cell Painting assay (CPA) is an image-based procedure that utilizes several dyes to stain various cellular components and allows for the extraction of hundreds to thousands of morphological features that are used to construct phenotypic profiles of treatment conditions. However, achieving significant throughput with the CPA is often met with challenges including image acquisition speed and scalability in a high throughput format. Here, we optimized the CPA for the Araceli Endeavor®- a high speed and high content imaging platform that generates high resolution images fit for morphological profiling. Our modified CPA utilized of a panel of five cellular stains captured in the four separate fluorescent channels of the Endeavor®. With the Endeavor® platform, we consistently capture high-quality staining of U-2 OS cells in both CellVis 384-well and Greiner 1536-well plates in under 10 minutes. We also demonstrate our ability to characterize distinct morphological profiles from a panel of reference chemicals used in Willis, et al. 2020 in both 384 and 1536 plate densities. Ultimately, we demonstrate the success of performing our modified CPA in both 384- and 1536-well plates and utilizing the high-content images from the Endeavor® to construct morphological profiles of various compounds with known mechanisms of action.
5 - In Silico Labeling Enables Accurate Phenotypic Profiling for Hit Discovery and Hit Expansion
Presenter's name
Jian Fang
Company/Institution
Novartis
Category
Advancements in Image-Based AI & Foundation Models for Drug Discovery
In silico labeling (ISL) is a computational microscopy technique leveraging GenAI to predict fluorescent staining from brightfield (BF) images. By virtually multiplexing multiple fluorescent readouts, ISL offers substantial cost-savings that expand capabilities for high- content imaging. However, critical questions regarding ISL’s accuracy in phenotypic profiling remain underexplored. This study systematically evaluates ISL’s performance across 50,000 diverse compounds in key drug discovery scenarios. First, we investigated ISL’s accuracy in predicting phenotype modulation across varied perturbagens. This included training on ~400 compounds with known mechanism of action from 10 fluorescence probes, and validation on 50,000 diverse compounds. ISL reliably predicted 10 imaging channels from BF inputs, showing over 80% overlap with ground-truth staining in unsupervised hit identification. Virtual multiplexing by ISL also revealed novel probe- specific hits, capturing subtle phenotypes beyond stained channels. Second, we evaluated ISL’s ability to predict bioactivity by retrospectively benchmarking ISL-generated profiles for hit expansion against 270 historical primary assays. Orthogonal imaging fingerprints derived from ISL achieved a 4-fold average increase in hit rate enrichment for cell-based assays and a 2-fold increase for cell-free assays. ISL also demonstrated complementarity to Morgan fingerprints, robust correlation with staining-based methods, that enhanced hit diversity through multiplexed imaging fingerprints. This study showcases ISL as a powerful tool for phenotypic profiling and drug discovery, with scalable accuracy and enriched bioactivity predictions across distinct assay modalities. As ISL continues to mature, it has the potential to become a cornerstone of modern drug discovery to enable efficient biological fingerprinting for hit identification and expansion.

Category

Rare genetic diseases present unique challenges due to limited access to patient cells and a lack of cellular and animal models. Niemann–Pick type C (NPC) disease is a rare and fatal neurodegenerative disorder (1:150,000) caused by mutations in the gene encoding the NPC1 protein responsible for intracellular lipid trafficking. These mutations lead to large lysosomal accumulation of unesterified cholesterol and cellular damage that can be detected by high-content imaging technologies. We recently developed a single plasmid technology for CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing that facilitates the generation of cellular models of human diseases. The system allows multiplex insertion of labeling tags into endogenous genes for protein detection and the insertion of targeted mutations at specific genomic regions. This enables the development of cellular models for high-content imaging drug discovery in live cells without the use of chemical staining or immunofluorescence. In this study, we created a cellular model of NPC disease using HeLa cells by employing the one plasmid system for genome editing. We introduced the most common mutation of NPC1 and tagged three endogenous proteins with fluorescent markers to detect subcellular structures in live cells. We confirmed large phenotypic differences in the number, size, and signal intensity of lysosomes in NPC1 mutated cells. We used the novel NPC cellular model to screen a library of autophagy modulators and identified compounds that reduce lysosomal enlargement and accumulation. In conclusion, this model accurately recapitulates the lysosomal accumulation phenotype observed in NPC patients and can be used for high-content imaging-based drug discovery.

The exponential increase in biomedical data, including multi-omics and high-content phenotypic data, offers unprecedented opportunities for understanding biological mechanisms and accelerating drug discovery, yet the sheer amount of data overwhelms traditional analysis methods. Here we introduce a framework for autonomous artificial intelligence (AI)-driven drug discovery that integrates knowledge graphs with large language models (LLMs). This system is capable of planning and carrying out automated data mining and data analysis programs to make novel discoveries, develop new hypotheses, and iteratively test those hypotheses using vast quantities of public or private data. Throughout this process, the system provides transparent details of its research strategies, progress, and supporting data, enabling a thorough assessment of its methods and findings by human researchers. We present an autonomous, agentic focal-graph based platform that can analyze cell painting and multi-omics data, yielding novel insights relevant to multiple stages of biomedical research and drug discovery.
9 - Dual morphological profiling approaches to characterize schizophrenia in an in vitro placental model
Presenter's name
Frank Piscotta
Company/Institution
Lieber Institute
Category
Advancements in Image-Based AI & Foundation Models for Drug Discovery
Trophoblast stem cells (TSCs) represent a powerful tool for the in vitro study of placental development and function. As placental health has been shown to be critical in brain development and potential risk for schizophrenia (SCZ), we leveraged Cell Painting to profile 23 genotypically distinct TSC lines derived from human patient iPSCs from SCZ (13 lines) and non-SCZ donors (10 lines). Imaging data was used to generate both traditional (CellProfiler) and CNN-derived feature profiles. Profiles were used to train Random Forest classifiers of sex and diagnosis (Dx), two attributes likely driving line-to-line morphological differences. Sex classifiers showed strong performance as measured by ROC AUC with 10-fold cross validation (median AUC>0.87). Mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum granularity showed significant decreases in male lines as compared to females, indicating highly salient sexual dimorphism in TSCs. Dx classifiers also showed good performance (median AUC~0.8). Interestingly, nuclear features, including several related to texture and shape (e.g., solidity), comprised many of the top features driving Dx classification. Finally, we examined the effect of hypoxia, a placental stressor, on sex and Dx classifiers and observed significant changes in feature importance: notably, nuclear area/shape features were no longer among the most important for Dx classification, suggesting that hypoxia may induce cellular changes that obfuscate certain morphological differences between SCZ and control cells. Collectively, these findings indicate that image-based profiling using Cell Painting can identify subtle morphological changes in a genetically diverse set of TSC lines derived from patients with SCZ, along with changes induced by physiologically significant disturbances.

12 - Revitalizing Structural Similarity for Bright-field to Fluorescence Microscopy Image Translation  
Presenter's name
Jingzhe Ma
Company/Institution
Xellar Bio
Category
Advancements in Image-Based AI & Foundation Models for Drug Discovery
Virtually translating bright-field images to fluorescence images can enhance efficiency and avoid dye toxicity. However, prior pixel-wise and conventional perceptual loss functions often fail to capture fine structural and contrast details, resulting in blurred outputs. To overcome these limitations, we propose a novel Multi-Scale Contrast-Structure Consistency Loss (MS-CSL) that revitalizes the traditional SSIM loss by focusing solely on the contrast and structure components while discarding the luminance component. This design is motivated by our observation that contrast and structure play a crucial role in downstream tasks, whereas luminance has minimal impact.
