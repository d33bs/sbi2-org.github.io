1 — JUMP-lite: Compact, reproducible benchmarking of cell representations
Poster Number
1
Applicant Name
Alán Fernando Muñoz González
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Image-based profiling captures rich phenotypic signatures for drug discovery and functional genomics. Large public datasets like JUMP Cell Painting now provide millions of images for systematic study. However, the scale of these resources—115 TB for JUMP alone—and fragmented evaluation practices make systematic comparison of representation methods intractable for many researchers. Here, we present Nahual, an open-source framework for reproducible model deployment, and JUMP-lite, a curated 92 GB subset of JUMP that is 1,200 times smaller while preserving phenotypic diversity through careful selection of perturbations with high-confidence annotations and reduced storage requirements via lossy JPEG XL compression. Using these resources, we benchmark five representation methods, including classical features (CellProfiler) and deep learning models (MorphEM, OpenPhenom, SubCell, and DINOv2). We demonstrate that compression preserves downstream signal, while standardized phenotypic activity and consistency metrics reveal meaningful performance differences across methods. Together, JUMP-lite and Nahual provide a foundation for accessible, reproducible benchmarking of image-based cell representations.
2 — Live Organoid Screening with SCAPE:Scaling Throughput for Light Sheet Imaging
Poster Number
2
Applicant Name
Andrew Barazia
Category
Acquisition. The Next Generation of Imaging Technologies
Light sheet microscopy features fast, gentle live imaging, but is traditionally limited in throughput due to the need for specialized sample mounting. The special geometry of single-objective light sheet systems permits the use of standard carriers such as slides and well plates, thus enabling high-throughput 3D imaging with standard sample preparation. Implementation of SCAPE (Swept, Confocally-Aligned Planar Excitation) microscopy promotes rapid volumetric imaging through continuous sweeping of the light sheet without the need for sample movement. In this poster, we demonstrate how SCAPE facilitates (a) capturing of rapid dynamics, (b) gentle long-term imaging of live samples at high temporal resolution without photodamage, and (c) large-scale imaging of developing specimens in multiwell formats. We present data from organoid cultures imaged in multiwell plates over several days for 3D morphological characterization. Two human organoid systems were compared: patient-derived neuroendocrine tumor organoids (PDTOs) and healthy fetal airway organoids enriched in pulmonary neuroendocrine cells (PNECs). Comparing morphology, actin organization, and tight junction-associated proteins; distinct phenotypes emerged, ranging from organized lumen-forming epithelia to compact, mechanically rigid structures in PDTOs. Together, these findings enable the exploration between cellular mechanosensation to emergent organoid architecture in healthy and malignant contexts. As a pilot study, this work establishes SCAPE’s ability to scale model system screening to volumetric live imaging, paving the way for disease modeling and drug-response profiling in 3D and over time.
3 — METTRO: High-Dimensional Live-Cell Morphological Profiling of Phenotypic Trajectories
Poster Number
3
Applicant Name
Emily Stoops
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Cells are exquisitely sensitive to changes in their environment, modulating their states and behaviors in response to detection of nutrients, cues, and stressors. Many analytical methods provide quantitative, unbiased descriptions of cell states. However, only microscopy-based measurements of cell morphology offer straightforward compatibility with longitudinal, live-cell measurements of single-cells at scale. Despite this, most morphological cell profiling is done using fixed time point imaging rather than longitudinal imaging. Here, we present an approach to high-throughput live-cell morphological profiling, paired with an analytical framework called Morphological Ensembles of Trajectories from Time Resolved Observations (METTRO) for establishing high-dimensional representations of phenotypic trajectories. We apply this method to studying the dynamics of the integrated stress response, a fundamental process that modulates protein synthesis in response to diverse cellular stressors. Longitudinal imaging enables the quantitative description of dynamic single-cell responses to perturbation, such as the rate of response and the rate of recovery from stress. Study of heterogeneity in the trajectories of individual cells allow us to understand the implications of intrinsic heterogeneity of these responses on life or death outcomes.
4 — A Vision-Gene-Language Model for Inferring Spatial Molecular Features from Routine Histopathology
Poster Number
4
Applicant Name
Dongjoo Lee
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Background: Spatial transcriptomics (ST) provides molecular information within tissue context but remains limited by cost, assay availability, and tissue requirements. Because most retrospective clinical cohorts rely on routine H&E slides, we investigated whether molecular features learned from spatial omics could be transferred to histopathology using a multimodal vision-language model. Methods: VGL-stGen was developed by QLoRA fine-tuning of MedGemma-1.5-4b-it using a patient-derived lung cancer atlas integrating spatial transcriptomics, single-cell transcriptomics, and pathology images. Spatial molecular measurements were transformed into image- and text-based representations, enabling a single model to learn multiple tasks linking tissue morphology, gene expression, and cellular identity, including cancer detection, cell-type classification, and gene activity prediction. Cancer detection was externally evaluated on TCGA lung adenocarcinoma (TCGA-LUAD) pathology slides, while H&E-derived gene activity profiles were compared with matched bulk RNA-sequencing measurements. Results: VGL-stGen performed multiple image-, gene-, and cell-based tasks within a unified model. On external datasets, H&E-based cancer detection was comparable to dedicated pathology foundation encoders, while transcriptome-based cell-type classification was comparable to task-specific and single-cell foundation model baselines. The model also recovered molecular information directly from H&E beyond chance-level prediction. In TCGA-LUAD, predicted gene activity profiles showed reproducible correspondence with independently measured bulk RNA data, with gene-program-level signals showing greater stability than individual genes. Conclusion: VGL-stGen provides a unified framework connecting histopathology, gene expression, and cellular identity. By transferring molecular information learned from ST to routine H&E images, this approach may extend molecular and spatially informed analyses to large retrospective pathology cohorts where direct spatial measurements are unavailable.
5 — On doubting image quality assessment metrics for microscopy virtual staining
Poster Number
5
Applicant Name
Weishan Li
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Virtual staining could reduce the cost and experimental burden of fluorescence microscopy, but its utility depends on generalization beyond the training domain. Most studies evaluate virtual staining using image quality assessment (IQA) metrics developed for natural images, yet it remains unclear whether these metrics faithfully reflect microscopy image quality. We evaluated seven commonly used full-reference objectives and metrics–MAE, PSNR, SSIM, foreground PSNR and SSIM, LPIPS, and DISTS–using controlled degradations of Cell Painting images spanning 18 cell lines, five seeding densities, and five fluorescence channels. Channel, cell line, and seeding density explained substantial metric variation even after accounting for degradation severity. DISTS and foreground metrics showed a more favorable balance between degradation sensitivity and biological invariance, but no metric was truly independent of biological context. Across graded degradations, most metrics occupied only a limited portion of their nominal ranges and often plateaued while visible image deterioration continued. We next trained UNet, WGAN-GP, and UNeXt virtual staining models on U2-OS cells across five seeding densities and evaluated predictions across 17 unseen cell lines. Architecture and training density together explained less than 2% of metric variation, while similar scores could correspond to qualitatively distinct failures in morphology and marker intensity. These findings suggest that conventional IQA metrics incompletely characterize virtual staining performance. Application-oriented evaluation that tests whether predictions preserve biologically relevant conclusions may provide a more meaningful alternative.
6 — Tracking 3D Cell Surface Dynamics with Physics-Guided Surface Correspondence
Poster Number
6
Applicant Name
Mou Deb
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Cells constantly move and remodel their surfaces to perform essential biological functions, including immune surveillance, wound repair, and cancer invasion. Surface deformation actively shapes how cells sense, signal, and interact with their environment. Though algorithms exist for tracking cell shape dynamics in 2D movies, these approaches do not readily extend to tracking 3D cell surfaces. We developed two physics-guided approaches for tracking 3D cell surfaces: a deep learning pipeline and an alternative discrete optimization pipeline. Both establish dense point-to-point correspondences between consecutive surfaces, enabling membrane locations to be tracked over time to quantify local surface displacement, curvature changes, and transient morphological events. However, tracking deformable surfaces is inherently under-specified, and manual annotation is impractical. To address this challenge, we use functional maps, a compact spectral representation of surface correspondences, with customizable physics-guided constraints that favor preservation of local area, position, curvature, and structural consistency while allowing biological remodeling. The deep learning pipeline learns intrinsic surface features to improve correspondence estimation, while the discrete optimization pipeline iteratively refines correspondences by alternating between functional-map and point-to-point domains. Using this framework, we tracked bleb dynamics on melanoma cells and mapped spatial patterns of signaling activity and their rates of change on evolving cell surfaces. These constraints also provide a way to investigate which physical principles explain observed surface dynamics. Together, these approaches provide a foundation for quantifying dynamic 3D cell surface behavior and its relationship to cellular signaling, with potential applications in quantitative phenotyping and characterizing drug-induced changes in cell morphology and dynamics.
7 — Paired longitudinal live-cell imaging and transcriptomics enable deeper characterization of cells
Poster Number
7
Applicant Name
Pier Federico Gherardini
Category
Acquisition. The Next Generation of Imaging Technologies
Imaging live cells produces rich information about cellular phenotypes. However, investigating regulatory programs that underlie observed behaviors is challenging due to the inability of conventional microscopy to provide genome-wide information on gene activity. To address this limitation, we developed CellCage Technology, which leverages advances in computer vision, electromechanical engineering, and hydrogel chemistry to enable longitudinal imaging of live cells coupled with endpoint transcriptomics at a scale of tens of thousands of cells per experiment. Within the context of this technology imaging data provides two complementary layers of information. On one hand imaging is a rich source of morphological descriptors that can be combined with gene expression information for analysis. We demonstrate this approach by leveraging DINOv2 embeddings derived from cellular images to cluster cells based on their morphological trajectories and identify different morphotypes associated with distinct molecular programs. On the other hand, fluorescent reporters can be used to derive precise functional measurements from imaging data. In multiple cellular systems including microglia phagocytosis and preadipocytes senescence and differentiation, we show that these functional endpoints can be used to build supervised models that identify the genetic drivers of cellular function in a way that is not possible using unsupervised analysis of transcriptomic data alone. In conclusion, we demonstrate that the combination of longitudinal live cell imaging and transcriptomics that is uniquely enabled by CellCage Technology provides a novel and unique datatype with the ability to better resolve cellular states and function within complex cellular populations.
8 — Stimulus-Specific Morphological Signature in Neutrophils Revealed by Cell Painting
Poster Number
8
Applicant Name
Ankit Bhatta
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Neutrophils are rapid, short-lived innate immune cells whose activation states influence host defense, inflammatory disease, and therapeutic response. Their transient biology and technical sensitivity, however, have limited systematic high-content profiling. Here, we applied Cell Painting, a multiplexed imaging method that captures cellular architecture through image-derived morphological features, to define stimulus-specific activation signatures in primary neutrophils. Neutrophils were exposed to six conditions: untreated control (NT), phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA), Nigericin, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), myeloperoxidase antibody plus tumor necrosis factor (MPO_Ab_TNF), and calcium ionophore A23189. High-content image features were analyzed using dimensionality reduction, machine-learning classification, and multi-metric similarity methods. UMAP visualization revealed distinct treatment-associated morphological distributions, demonstrating that neutrophil activation comprises diverse, stimulus-specific phenotypes. PMA and A23189 generated the most distinct signatures, consistent with their different mechanisms: PMA induces protein kinase C-dependent NETosis, whereas A23189 promotes calcium-dependent activation. LPS and MPO_Ab_TNF occupied similar regions of the embedding space, a finding supported by Pearson correlation, cosine similarity, Euclidean distance, and hierarchical clustering. Although these stimuli act through different receptors, their morphological convergence may reflect shared downstream remodeling involving innate immune and NETosis-related pathways. Random forest classification further showed that treatment groups could be distinguished from Cell Painting features. Mitochondrial measurements dominated the top feature rankings, implicating stimulus-specific changes in mitochondrial morphology, intensity, texture, and organization as a major axis of neutrophil activation. Overall, Cell Painting provides a scalable platform for generating morphological fingerprints of neutrophil states and may support disease-mechanism studies and screening for compounds that modulate neutrophil activation.

Acquisition. The Next Generation of Imaging Technologies
Genome organization and its regulation play a fundamental role in development and disease, with recent sequencing and imaging innovations providing novel insights into these mechanisms. Although Oligopaints, which are rationally designed probes for fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), were introduced to visualize spatial genome architecture at single-cell resolution, high costs have historically limited genome-wide screens for identifying regulatory factors. To overcome these barriers, we established high-throughput DNA or RNA labelling with optimized Oligopaints (HIDRO), an advanced imaging framework that integrates enhancements to probe design and FISH labeling workflows to facilitate scalable, high-throughput screening. 10 — OpenIMC: An Open-Source Platform for Analyzing Single-Cell and Spatial Proteomics by IMC
Poster Number
10
Applicant Name
Dean Tessone
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Spatially resolved single-cell proteomics enables quantitative characterization of tissue organization and molecular heterogeneity. Imaging Mass Cytometry (IMC) uses heavy-metal-tagged antibodies to generate up to 40-plex imaging with no spectral overlap at sub-cellular pixel resolution. IMC has enabled unparalleled description of cancer tissue architecture, revealing novel populations of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), describing prognostic cellular neighborhoods, and identifying that spatial organization is a predictor for therapy response in numerous indications. Despite its analytical power, IMC data analysis is fragmented across multiple software environments, requiring researchers to combine independent tools for visualization, preprocessing, segmentation, feature extraction, phenotyping, batch correction, and spatial analysis. This fragmentation increases technical barriers, complicates reproducibility, and limits accessibility for non-computational users. Further, existing tools are often optimized for only a single OS (i.e. MCD Viewer on Windows), limiting data sharing across teams. We developed OpenIMC, an open-source platform that integrates the major stages of IMC analysis within a unified graphical and command-line framework. OpenIMC supports image visualization, quality control, preprocessing, segmentation, feature extraction, dimensionality reduction, batch effect correction, clustering, phenotyping, and spatial analysis while maintaining interoperability with established community tools. OpenIMC builds on the paradigm of reproducible image analysis established by CellProfiler, extending this framework to spatial proteomics.
11 — From images to experiment: language-model augmented approaches to interpret optical pooled screens
Poster Number
11
Applicant Name
Matteo Di Bernardo
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
To unlock the function of all human genes, we must perturb each and observe the consequence in cells. Optical pooled screens do this by pairing CRISPR perturbations with microscopy: each cell is imaged and its guide read in situ, linking perturbation to phenotype across millions of cells. We previously built Brieflow, the first open-source pipeline taking terabytes of images to human-interpretable features and phenotypic clusters. With OttoSeq, which automates reagent dispensing for in situ sequencing, we executed a genome-wide screen, including Brieflow analysis, in one week. Yet interpreting these data to design follow-up experiments remains human-intensive, increasingly so as functional genomics experiments accumulate. This requires the latest literature on each gene's function, a reading of every cluster of related knockouts, and context across screens. Here, mirroring the steps a scientist takes, we present a language-model augmented approach to interpret screens at scale. First, Affinage reads the primary literature and writes a cited mechanistic record for genes genome-wide, consistently outperforming human-curated databases. Second, on Brieflow's output, MozzareLLM annotates every cluster with a pathway, a calibrated confidence and a per-gene category, flagging uncharacterized genes and known genes in unexpected places (Fig. 1). Third, MasCarPone places multiple screens and marker panels behind a Model Context Protocol server, letting researchers and agents query this growing compendium in plain language (Fig. 2). We have begun following up on these results, and envision that, as we and others run more screens, this approach can deliver on the promise of functional genomics to de-orphan the human genome.
12 — High-Content Cell Painting Platform Identifies a Reproducible Morphological Signature of SELENON-CM
Poster Number
12
Applicant Name
Pamela Barraza-Flores
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
SELENON-Congenital Myopathy (SELENON-CM) is a rare neuromuscular disease caused by recessive mutations in the SELENON gene and characterized by muscle weakness, progressive axial muscle rigidity, spinal deformities, and respiratory insufficiency. Selenon-deficient mouse and zebrafish models present with mild to no phenotypes, hindering therapeutic development. To address this gap, we established a high-content Cell Painting platform using newly developed human immortalized myoblasts comprising wild-type (WT) and two independent SELENON-KO clones. Morphological profiling generated >3,000 feature measurements describing nuclear morphology, texture, and spatial organization of multiple cellular compartments. Features altered in the same direction in both SELENON-KO clones relative to WT were used to define a SELENON-deficient morphological signature. To test whether this signature reflects SELENON-dependent biology, we restored SELENON expression using a muscle-tropic AAV vector (MyoAAV-SELENON), identifying a reproducible 47-feature signature. We next applied this validated signature to a pilot pharmacological screen of four compounds across multiple concentrations. Drug responses were quantified relative to vehicle, and rescue required the same feature to shift toward WT in both SELENON-KO clones. CDN1163 at 0.3 µM produced the broadest response, shifting 36 of 47 (76.6%) validated features toward WT in both clones. Other compounds produced fewer features shifting toward WT. Together, these data establish a reproducible high-content morphological phenotype of SELENON-deficient cellular models and provide the basis for a high-content phenotypic screening assay for SELENON-CM, with potential applications to therapeutic discovery in other rare neuromuscular diseases.

14 — Defining the genetic determinants of micronucleus formation using optical pooled screening
Poster Number
14
Applicant Name
Louise Morlot
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Chromosomal instability resulting from defective genome maintenance underlies numerous diseases, including cancer. Micronuclei (MN), a hallmark of malignant cells arising due to mitotic errors or DNA damage, represent a particularly strong manifestation and source of chromosomal instability, driving cancer, pro-inflammatory signaling, and senescence. The deleterious impacts of MN have been linked to the abnormal rearrangement of their genetic material and rupture of the micronuclear envelope, leading to chromothripsis via reintegration of the micronuclear DNA into the main nucleus. Despite rapidly growing insights into their consequences, the genetic determinants of MN formation have not been globally profiled due to the shortcomings of conventional CRISPR screens for correlating morphological and spatial readouts with genetic profiles of individual cells. To overcome this limitation, we leveraged an optical pooled screening (OPS) approach coupling genome-scale targeted CRISPR perturbations decoded by in situ sequencing with multiplexed microscopy-based quantification of MN formation at single cell level. To this end, we developed mnDINO, a new deep learning model based on Vision Transformers, to segment MN with unmatched confidence across millions of individual cells. Integrating OPS and mnDINO, we established the first genome-wide map of genes antagonizing spontaneous MN formation in human cells. CRISPRi-based validation screens and follow-up studies provided further characterization of hits, recapitulating known genetic determinants of MN formation and uncovering novel pathways and factors suppressing micronucleation. Collectively, these data resources advance the understanding of mechanisms suppressing the formation and pathological potential of MN, with implications for the development of novel cancer therapies.

16 — Doubling Markers per Channel and Removing Background Interference in Fluorescence Microscopy
Poster Number
16
Applicant Name
Ivana Mikic
Category
Acquisition. The Next Generation of Imaging Technologies
Fluorescence imaging is limited by channel capacity and background interference (autofluorescence, channel crosstalk, and non-specific staining). We present two software tools that address both limits without changing hardware or reagents. Sarpeda Plex separates two markers labeled with the same fluorophore, doubling the markers per acquisition. Sarpeda Clear removes background learned from negative controls. Both problems are formulated as unpaired image-to-image translation, so training needs no pixel-aligned pairs. Plex requires one conventional sample per tissue type and marker pair, with the markers imaged separately. Clear trains on negative controls. Architectural and training innovations prevent the hallucinations typical of unpaired generative models and preserve the fidelity of biological content. Plex separates E-cadherin and Ki-67 acquired in one channel in breast tissue, matching a serial section with the markers imaged separately (Fig 1). On a benchmark paired dataset Plex performs on par with a state-of-the-art paired model, while that model, trained on summed images, fails on real mixed images. Clear recovers E-cadherin signal buried in strong lung autofluorescence from a negative control alone (Fig 2). On mouse brain serial sections, corrected images closely match independent ground truth, and the result holds across different sections and different negative controls. Clear removes channel crosstalk, so all channels can be acquired at once rather than sequentially, and Plex doubles the markers each channel carries. On a four-camera high-content system this means 8 markers imaged on a single sample simultaneously, at the quality of sequential imaging.
17 — High-Content Imaging of Human Astrovirus Identifies Repurposed Antivirals with Distinct Phenotypes
Poster Number
17
Applicant Name
Samantha Boisvert
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Human astrovirus (HAstV) is a common cause of acute and potentially serious viral gastroenteritis worldwide, yet no approved antivirals exist. Here, we report the first large-scale phenotypic drug repurposing screen against HAstV using high-content imaging. Cell-level features enabled machine learning classification of viral infection while simultaneously capturing cellular phenotypes that provide insight into mode of action. 4,494 compounds were screened in Caco-2 cells, resulting in 72 hits. Of these, 56 were confirmed concentration-responsive with potencies as low as 33.8 nM, and 11 were selected for qPCR validation and time-of-addition profiling based on structural and host-directed pathway diversity. Notably, a subset of hits, including our positive control, produced a striking redistribution of viral capsid signal toward the nuclear compartment, distinct from the predominantly cytoplasmic localization of capsid during infection. We then used UMAP as a complementary hit-calling approach to identify additional compounds sharing this phenotype that were missed by conventional scoring. We confirmed spatial overlap of the viral and nuclear signals using 60X Nyquist-spaced imaging, and control experiments in the absence of virus ruled out host- and compound-antibody interactions. Compounds producing this phenotype were structurally and mechanistically diverse suggesting they may converge on a specific aspect of the virus biology. These results demonstrate how phenotypic drug repurposing can identify antivirals with potential for rapid clinical translation, provide starting points for drug development, and reveal unexpected phenotypes that point to new biology.

20 — Control-Aware Embedding Selection for Quality Control in Image-Based Profiling
Poster Number
20
Applicant Name
Daniel Siegismund
Category
Analysis. From Observations to Novel Phenotypes
Low-dimensional embeddings are widely used to explore quality-control image-based profiling data, including Cell Painting. Yet the choice between methods such as UMAP, PHATE, and t-SNE is often guided by convention rather than evidence. Methods are commonly compared using structural quality metrics, such as neighborhood preservation, which assumes that a structurally faithful embedding is also biologically informative. Here, we ask whether embeddings selected for structural fidelity also maximize biological detectability in image-based profiling quality control. We benchmarked eight embedding methods on the public JUMP-CP resource, using roughly 9,100 profiles from compound-based perturbations and excluding ORF and CRISPR perturbations. We assessed both local and global structure preservation and tested whether each embedding separated two biologically and mechanistically distinct positive controls from DMSO: RKI-1447, a ROCK1/2 inhibitor affecting cytoskeletal organization, and BI-2536, a PLK1 inhibitor causing mitotic arrest. BI-2536 was more distant from DMSO than RKI-1447. Tuning a regularized t-SNE from local toward global structure via DREAMS degraded neighborhood preservation by roughly an order of magnitude, while BI-2536 became about five times easier to detect and detection of RKI-1447 was unaffected. These results show that structural quality is not a reliable proxy for biological detectability: optimizing neighborhood preservation reduced detection of the mitotic phenotype while leaving detection of the cytoskeletal phenotype unchanged. Selecting Cell Painting embeddings on neighborhood-preservation scores alone can therefore limit detection of desired phenotypes, even when those phenotypes are part of the assay design. Embedding choice should instead be validated against several mechanistically distinct controls that represent the intended readout.
21 — Closing the Loop: AI-Driven Next-Step Decisions from Brightfield Images for Autonomous Cell Culture
Poster Number
21
Applicant Name
Pavana Gururaj Rotti
Category
Application. Novel Phenotypes to Decisions
Background: Automation has transformed cell based workflows, yet deciding what to do next remains manual. Instruments run unattended, but responding to a culture's state still depends on expert review and slow, destructive endpoint molecular assays. This limits throughput and reproducibility. Methods: Nervoid Inc. developed an AI framework that converts label-free brightfield images directly into workflow decisions. Models were trained on imaging data from 20 cell types in 2D and 3D organoid cultures, at single-cell and multicellular scale. From brightfield images alone, the system infers toxicity, oxidative stress, gene expression and signaling pathway profiles. It combines these into one of 100–200 decision classes, which are sent directly to laboratory automation to trigger the next action. Results: On held-out data, the models predicted next-step decisions with 96% accuracy (95% CI ±4%; macro-F1 = 0.85). Customers reported 90% accuracy in real-world deployments. The models correctly identified the top 20 altered signaling pathways. Predicted differential gene expression agreed with transcriptomic ground truth (Pearson r = 0.82; AUROC = 0.75; Benjamini–Hochberg FDR < 0.05).
